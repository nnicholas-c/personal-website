EECS at UC Berkeley, interested in agentic AI x deeptech x semiconductors

I build software for servers and study the decisions people keep making.

Most of my time at Berkeley goes to two things. The first is Clastra, a company I started to give every server its own AI agent. The agent is a small model that runs on the machine and looks after everything on it, and you can reach any of them from one website.

The second is research. With an operations lab at Berkeley Haas, I study repeated decisions, the kind people make dozens of times a day at work. In our pilot, workers packed too many orders into each trip because they priced their own time at about half its value, and I'm testing what kind of AI help actually changes that.

Before Berkeley I spent a long time on olympiads. I went to the IOI national camp, reached USACO Gold, and worked on machine learning for molecular design with the Pentelute Lab at MIT.

On the quant side, one of my results came out negative. A stock signal that backtested at +8% a year fell to -0.7% once I rebuilt the data point-in-time. It was survivorship bias, and I wrote it up anyway.

Selected research

Human decisions, reinforcement learning, and machine learning for molecules. The workshop paper and the molecular design paper are mine alone. The rest are joint work from the operations lab at Berkeley Haas.

  1. The Withdrawal TestCHI 2027, submitted

    With 118 MBA students, adding a rationale to an optimal recommendation did not show a lasting benefit once the help was taken away.

  2. When Personalization Feels AccurateCHI 2027, submitted

    Professionals rated learned accounts of their own judgment highly, even accounts that predicted the same verdict for every held-out draft.

  3. Personalized Reward Models Can Agree for the Wrong ReasonsICLR 2027, submitted

    When you model someone's judgment, agreement rate alone cannot tell good reasoning from lucky reasoning.

  4. Agreeing for the Wrong Reasons: Coordinate Identifiability in Personalized Reward ModelsAISTATS 2027, under review

    A reward model can predict a person's accept or reject choices well without learning what its named factors mean, and labels for the reason behind a rejection help recover it.

  5. RuleRevealAAAI 2027, under review

    Two agents can tie on a benchmark and still act very differently, so RuleReveal picks the few test problems that best tell them apart.

  6. Approval Is Not DiscoveryNeurIPS 2026 AgenticLS workshop

    An AI discovery agent that earns autonomy by agreeing with a scientist may only be copying the scientist's quick check.

  7. AI-driven molecular designMIT Pentelute Lab, peer-reviewed

    Diffusion models plus reinforcement learning, aimed at molecules that bind well without being toxic.
